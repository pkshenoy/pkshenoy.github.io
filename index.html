After decades of nodding solemnly at seriousness and pretending the world actually makes sense, I've finally traded my briefcase for a quill dipped in delicious absurdity. Retirement didn't silence my mind — it unleashed the inner jester who'd been itching to laugh at the spectacular circus we call reality.

Now, with no boss but my caffeine cravings, I dive headfirst into the tangled spaghetti of human behavior, unmasking the ironies, hypocrisies, and glorious contradictions we all awkwardly tiptoe around. No absurdity is safe from my pen.

My writing is a playground where wit and wisdom collide, logic does somersaults, seriousness gets a well-deserved timeout, and satire reigns supreme — like a clown wearing a crown. Whether it's the bureaucracy of bananas, emotional weather reports, or the existential crisis of a sock missing its partner, there's always something worth a raised eyebrow.

This isn't just humor — it's philosophy with a tickle.

The tiny manifesto

A few things I try to remember.

Not commandments. More like friendly notes left for a mind that has a habit of wandering off with the scissors.

  1. 01

    Curiosity over certainty.

    Being willing to ask is usually more useful than pretending to know. The question mark remains the most honest punctuation.

  2. 02

    Nothing is too sacred to be tickled.

    Satire isn't cruelty; it's a magnifying glass with a sense of humor. Seriousness can take a well-deserved timeout.

  3. 03

    Laugh at the absurdity, not the person.

    The world supplies plenty of material. Kindness is what keeps the punchline in the right place.

  4. 04

    Stay free enough to change your mind.

    Unconventional isn't a uniform. It's permission to think for yourself — even when the answer turns out to be surprisingly ordinary.
